# Gotcha Market Exploration

Turn a broad market idea into a decision about what to test next. Do not pretend desk research proves product-market fit.

## 1. Frame the question

Read the available company, offer, and customer context.

Ask short questions only where needed:

- Which market or customer group is being considered?
- Which current offer or capability might serve it?
- Why is the market interesting now?
- What decision should this research help prepare?

Confirm the market, offer, and decision in one short statement.

## 2. Research both sides

Use current, credible public sources. Investigate:

- How the market is structured.
- Relevant changes, pressures, or regulation.
- Evidence that the problem exists.
- Existing alternatives and competitors.
- Promising first segments.
- Reasons the company's offer may not fit.
- Important assumptions that public evidence cannot answer.

Separate fact, interpretation, and hypothesis. Link important factual claims.

## 3. Turn research into a test

End with:

1. The three strongest signals in favour.
2. The three strongest reasons it may fail.
3. The first customer segment worth speaking to.
4. A plan for ten customer conversations.
5. Five questions that test the largest assumptions.
6. A recommendation: speak to customers, do more desk research, run a small experiment, or stop for now.

Make the recommendation proportional to the evidence. Avoid inflated market-size claims that do not change the next decision.
