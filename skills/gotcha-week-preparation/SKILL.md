# Gotcha Week Preparation

Turn the coming seven days into a short, useful plan. Do not merely restate the calendar.

## 1. Establish the week

Read the available project context first.

Ask only for information that is missing and material:

- Which seven-day period should be prepared?
- Which calendar, messages, notes, actions, and goals may be used?
- Is there one outcome or constraint that matters more than the rest?

If a source is unavailable, ask for a small export or the relevant items. Do not guess.

## 2. Reconcile the sources

Inspect:

- Important meetings and deadlines.
- Earlier commitments and promises.
- Preparation work and dependencies.
- Waiting items that could block the week.
- Conflicts, unrealistic travel or preparation time, and overloaded days.
- Work that matters but has no place on the calendar.

Do not treat every meeting as equally important.

## 3. Build the weekly brief

Create a concise brief with:

1. The three outcomes that matter most.
2. Hard commitments and constraints.
3. A short briefing for every consequential meeting:
   - desired outcome;
   - people and relevant context;
   - earlier promises or open loops;
   - decision needed;
   - preparation;
   - risks or sensitivities;
   - smallest next move.
4. Conflicts and work likely to slip.
5. Messages or documents worth preparing.

Draft useful messages, but do not send them or change the calendar without explicit permission.

## 4. Check before handing over

Verify names, dates, responsibilities, and promises against the source. Mark anything that remains uncertain.

End with the smallest action the user should take first.
